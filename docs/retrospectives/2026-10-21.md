## 11. Practical lessons

1. **Test what the model was actually told, not only what a scripted response can satisfy.** Delivery and declaration sufficiency are separate tests; a shallow schema can arrive perfectly and still omit the consumer's rules.
2. **Keep five boundaries explicit:** JSON parsing, declared structure, contextual references, substantive judgment, authorized effect. A pass at one is not a pass at the next; a blocked effect does not make advice correct.
3. **A negative experiment can be a completed useful deliverable.** Preserve it, review its fidelity and save its factual limits. Do not buy another answer merely to obtain a green acceptance.
4. **Do not let a packet's stop condition become a campaign-wide prohibition.** End the finished worker invocation, then classify the original remainder as ready, awaiting an owner, technically uncertain or missing authority. If nothing can proceed, say exactly why and give a concrete decision, not a copied waiting label.
5. **Carry direct grants and cumulative liabilities through continuations.** A reminder is neither a new budget nor a revocation. Keep currencies, native reported charges, conservative reserves and unknown invoices separate.
6. **Trust actual serialization and physical custody over configuration and exit labels.** The cap, reasoning and363-F1 failures all lived beyond a superficially plausible local state.
7. **Reuse unchanged accepted evidence, but preserve genuine failures.** A missing fixture resource or wrong observer is not a product defect; an actual missed deadline is. Narrow same-owner repairs and independent rechecks worked better than reopening accepted stages.
8. **Saving, accepting and delivering are separate obligations.** Local commits did not prove a push; creating Repomix did not attach it; a tag did not publish a package. Source-bound receipts and explicit user-facing delivery close those gaps.
